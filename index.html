Session ZeroThe Longest Night▾
TL;DR

Session Zero sets the stage. Five years ago the Amaroon Enclave fell in Waterdeep, and on the same evening Etrian's kill switch device detonated over the Seven Hills, a Lisbon-inspired region twenty miles south. The Longest Night held the region beneath a violet veil for 77 hours, killed no one, and left behind permanent twilight, unstable magic, haunted landmarks, and the objects called Arcane Scars. Five years on, PIDE's surveillance state tightens in the north while the south survives on community, and old questions about the Enclave and the source of the Longest Night are surfacing again. -Claude

Party at level 5.

Campaign Setup

  • Characters start with a magic or rare item.

What in the Seven Hills? (DM Notes)

Five years ago, the Amaroon Enclave fell in Waterdeep.

On the same summer evening, far to the south, the Seven Hills went dark. A blinding flash rose beyond the Tagus. The shockwave knocked people from their feet, shattered windows, and silenced a region in an instant. Then came the darkness: a violet-black veil that swallowed the sky and held the Seven Hills beneath it for seventy-seven hours. No one died.

That is the part no one can explain. For three days and three nights, the world became strange and still. People gathered in homes, taverns, temples, warehouses, and public squares. They waited together beneath a sky without stars or sun, listening to an unfamiliar hum in their dreams and hoping the light would return.

When it did, the Seven Hills had changed.

The western coast was left in permanent twilight. Forests began growing in impossible shapes. Patches of land became unstable, where magic flickered, memory failed, and the world itself seemed unsure how it was meant to behave. Strange objects—later called Arcane Scars—began turning up in homes, markets, ruins, and the hands of people who could not explain where they had found them.

The Longest Night ended.

Its echo did not.

The Event

  • A mushroom cloud erupted roughly 20 miles south of Waterdeep, in the region known as the Seven Hills.
  • Some kind of moonstone explosion, as a result of identifying the shard.
  • Etrian's kill switch bomb; Zircon has the blueprint for the device.
  • The mushroom cloud did not cause any casualties. It just caused crazy things to happen: miscasting of spells, creatures of different planes of existence.
  • A purple shimmering mist covered the entire region for 77 hours.

The Lay of the Land (DM Notes)

The Seven Hills lies south of Waterdeep, where the broad Tagus meets the Sword Coast. It is a region divided by water, history, and increasingly by politics.

To the north stands Lissabon, the great city of seven hills: crowded streets, grand squares, harbours, noble houses, guild interests, and watchful authority. Once the region’s unquestioned heart, it has become a place of permits, checkpoints, data collection, and carefully managed public order. The Protection of Intellect and Data Enclave—PIDE—claims that its surveillance and restrictions are necessary to keep the population safe from the magical aftermath of the Longest Night.

West of the capital lie the green hills of Sintra and the coastal splendour of the Crystal Ward, where a glittering dome of unknown origin still imprisons part of the shoreline. Across the Tagus, the southern peninsula tells a different story.

Almada clings to the riverbank beneath the great bridge. The Clay Bay smokes with industry, labour, shipyards, and stubborn community. Beyond it stretch the darkened cliffs of the Shadow Shores, the unstable wilds of the Axium Forest, and the forested mountains of Arrábida—where few people willingly travel, and fewer return with the same certainty about what they saw.

The north speaks of order.

The south speaks of survival.

Between them flows the Tagus, carrying trade, rumours, refugees, secrets, and the quiet understanding that the Seven Hills is becoming two different worlds.

The Districts

  • A region of seven districts located 20 miles south of Waterdeep. Inspired by real-life Lisbon.
  • Per the Seven Hills map, the seven districts are Lissabon, Sintra, the Crystal Ward, Almada, the Clay Bay, the Shadow Shores, and Arrabida.
  • Lissabon:
    • The capital and great city of seven hills, under PIDE's watchful authority: permits, checkpoints, data collection, and carefully managed public order.
  • Sintra:
    • Green hills west of the capital. No other notes added yet.
  • Crystal Ward:
    • A dome was created over the region of the Crystal Ward after the mist, and possibly as a result of it.
    • The only way in or out is through a portal system, mainly used for food.
  • Almada:
    • Mostly a fishing town.
    • A very important port city.
  • The Clay Bay:
    • Smokes with industry, labour, shipyards, and stubborn community.
  • The Shadow Shores (formerly the Diamond Shores):
    • Went from a luxury spot to a place of eternal darkness, as a result of the mist shroud in the sky, even after five years.
    • Most inhabitants left the Shadow Shores for other regions.
  • Arrabida:
    • Home of the Emerald Enclave and the Warden-Kin.
    • Mainly a mountain range. Basically Maine.
    • Has an international port to travel from, operated by the Orders Gauntlet.
  • Also on the map: Troya, a ruin site to the southeast. No notes added yet.
  • Region-wide: many of the historic palaces and landmarks are now haunted.

The Echoes Remain (DM Notes)

Five years is long enough for a disaster to become routine.

People rebuild. Children grow up knowing which roads are unsafe after dark. Markets learn which Scar-touched objects are worth money and which ones whisper when no one is listening. Families adapt to strange weather, distorted memories, and the occasional patch of reality that refuses to behave. PIDE expands its reach in the name of protection. Communities south of the river endure because they have one another.

But something is changing. Arcane disturbances are becoming harder to ignore. Scarred objects are disappearing into official custody. People who ask the wrong questions are watched, discouraged, or simply vanish from public view. Old rumours about the Amaroon Enclave, its connections beyond Faerûn, and the source of the Longest Night have begun to surface once more.

The Seven Hills has lived with the consequences of the past for five years. Now it must decide what to do with them.

What truly caused the Longest Night? Why did no one die beneath its impossible darkness? What are the Arcane Scars—and why does PIDE want them? What did the Amaroon Enclave leave behind?

And if the region is still changing, how much of the Seven Hills can be lost before it is no longer home?

The Present Day

  • Nobody knows much.
  • Nobody knows where the Amaroon Enclave is.
  • PIDE is working similar to the CIA: it oversees the flow of any information and does all the investigating.
  • The government is a democratically elected right-wing authoritarian government with a good amount of surveillance.

Ended session at level 5.

AppendixThalume ("dome-light")▾

The tongue of the glowcap folk of the Veiled Forest, spoken by perhaps a few hundred individuals and never heard beyond the treeline. Spoken by Nimbus and his brother Lembas.

Learn Thalume →

Three Channels, One Language

  • Every full utterance in Thalume uses all three at once, the way humans use words, tone, and facial expression:
    • The Bloom (light). Meaning lives in patterns pulsed across the dome. Vocabulary is spatial: a word is a shape of light, its position on the dome acting like a case marking. Something displayed at the crown is a subject; something rippling toward the rim is an object or destination.
    • The Hum (sound). A soft resonance from the bell rim carries the grammar: tense, mood, and emotional register. The rim lights flicker in time with it, like a visible metronome.
    • The Thread (touch). Tendril-to-tendril contact is the intimate register of exact thoughts, used for true names, grief, and anything not meant for the wider grove. Two glowcaps with intertwined tendrils are having a private conversation in the middle of a public one. The more public display of this form of language is telepathic communication done at a distance, which is more formal and used for communicating with outsiders.

Phonology

  • Because glowcaps have no hard mouthparts, the hummed component has no plosives at all: no p, b, t, d, k, or g. Everything is made of soft continuants (m, n, l, s, sh, th, v, w, h) and long vowels.
  • Doubled vowels mark intensity or brightness: milu is "light," miluu is "blazing light."
  • The overall sound is somewhere between wind chimes and a cat's purr.

Tense Is a Color, Not a Word

TenseColorTranscription prefix
PastAmberaa-
PresentBlue-whiteee-
FutureVioletyy-
  • A glowcap describing something that never happened and never will simply goes dark mid-sentence.

There Is No Word for "I"

  • The smallest pronoun is venn, "we-of-two": an acknowledgment of oneness in connection during telepathic communication.
  • Glowcaps bud in sibling pairs and think of themselves as half of something. Nimbus, speaking of himself, would literally say "the Nimbus-half of venn."
  • A glowcap whose sibling has died uses the mourning pronoun ven-hollo, "we-of-two, one dark," for the rest of their life.
  • This makes "brother" nearly redundant in Thalume; the language assumes it.

There Is No Word for a Lie

  • Because of the abundance of telepathic communication, lying is nonexistent and nearly impossible to conceive for a glowcap.
  • They don't understand the purpose of a lie and see it merely as an inaccuracy, a sign of foolishness and ineffective communication. At that point, you might as well take the time and effort to read each other's minds.

Sample Phrases

  • Greeting a friend:

    Miluu venn shessa — "Brightly we-of-two join the grove."
    Delivered as a slow double-pulse at the crown of the dome, warm rim-hum, one tendril extended.

  • Greeting a stranger (wesshi):

    Aa-milu? — Roughly, "Was your light ever seen here before?"
    A cautious single pulse, low and amber.

Writing

  • Thalume script mimics the body: vertical hanging lines (tendrils) strung with dots and knots (light beads), read top to bottom.
  • A written word looks like a strand of tendrils with lights caught in it.
  • Books are woven curtains; reading one properly means running a tendril down each strand, so the script is as much braille as text.

True Names

## Thalume ("dome-light")
The tongue of the glowcap folk of the Veiled Forest, spoken by perhaps a few hundred individuals and never heard beyond the treeline. Spoken by Nimbus and his brother Lembas.

### Three Channels, One Language
Every full utterance in Thalume uses all three at once, the way humans use words, tone, and facial expression:
- **The Bloom (light).** Meaning lives in patterns pulsed across the dome. Vocabulary is spatial: a word is a shape of light, its position on the dome acting like a case marking. Something displayed at the crown is a subject; something rippling toward the rim is an object or destination.
- **The Hum (sound).** A soft resonance from the bell rim carries the grammar: tense, mood, and emotional register. The rim lights flicker in time with it, like a visible metronome.
- **The Thread (touch).** Tendril-to-tendril contact is the intimate register of exact thoughts, used for true names, grief, and anything not meant for the wider grove. Two glowcaps with intertwined tendrils are having a private conversation in the middle of a public one. The more public display of this form of language is telepathic communication done at a distance, which is more formal and used for communicating with outsiders.

### Phonology
Because glowcaps have no hard mouthparts, the hummed component has no plosives at all: no *p, b, t, d, k,* or *g*. Everything is made of soft continuants (*m, n, l, s, sh, th, v, w, h*) and long vowels.
- Doubled vowels mark intensity or brightness: *milu* is "light," *miluu* is "blazing light."
- The overall sound is somewhere between wind chimes and a cat's purr.

### Tense Is a Color, Not a Word
- Past: amber (*aa-*)
- Present: blue-white (*ee-*)
- Future: violet (*yy-*)
A glowcap describing something that never happened and never will simply goes dark mid-sentence.

### There Is No Word for "I"
The smallest pronoun is *venn*, "we-of-two": an acknowledgment of oneness in connection during telepathic communication.
- Glowcaps bud in sibling pairs and think of themselves as half of something. Nimbus, speaking of himself, would literally say "the Nimbus-half of *venn*."
- A glowcap whose sibling has died uses the mourning pronoun *ven-hollo*, "we-of-two, one dark," for the rest of their life.
- This makes "brother" nearly redundant in Thalume; the language assumes it.

### There Is No Word for a Lie
Because of the abundance of telepathic communication, lying is nonexistent and nearly impossible to conceive for a glowcap. They don't understand the purpose of a lie and see it merely as an inaccuracy, a sign of foolishness and ineffective communication. At that point, you might as well take the time and effort to read each other's minds.

### Lexicon
- *venn* — we-of-two (basic pronoun)
- *shessa* — the grove-mind; everyone currently in light-range
- *milu / miluu* — light / brilliant light
- *hollo* — darkness, absence; also silence
- *thal* — the dome or cap; by extension, the self's "face"
- *ume* — a pulse, a spoken thing, a word
- *nimm* — rain-haze, the soft glow of mist (root of Nimbus's name)
- *lemm* — nourishment shared freely, a gift of food (root of Lembas's name)
- *sylvenn* — the forest, literally "the many-of-two"
- *wesshi* — a stranger, literally "one whose light we cannot read"
- *hallume* — a story, literally "dark-and-light," since stories require both

### Sample Phrases

> *Miluu venn shessa* — "Brightly we-of-two join the grove."

**Greeting a stranger (*wesshi*):**
> *Aa-milu?* — Roughly, "Was your light ever seen here before?"
> A cautious single pulse, low and amber.
