Common phrases

The Echo Principle

Glowcaps have no flat yes or no. Agreement echoes the asker's light pattern back brighter. Refusal lets the pattern fade to dark. Uncertainty is a broken, flickering half-echo.

Tense is a color

aa-  past · amber ee-  present · blue-white yy-  future · violet

Going dark mid-sentence marks a thing that never happened and never will. Yy-hollo, a deliberately extinguished dome, is the gravest "never" a glowcap can give.

Three channels

The Bloom — light patterns on the dome carry vocabulary.

The Hum — rim resonance carries grammar and mood.

The Thread — tendril touch is the intimate register: true names, grief, and luvenn.

Sound rules

No plosives — no p, b, t, d, k, or g. Only soft continuants and long vowels.

Doubled vowels mean brightness or intensity: milu is light, miluu is blazing light.

How to say it

Vowels are always the same: a = "ah" · e = "eh" · i = "ee" · o = "oh" · u = "oo" · y = "yuh"

Hold a doubled vowel about twice as long: aa- is a long "aah," miluu ends on a held "ooo."

Doubled consonants are hummed or held — venn ends on a lingering hum, navv on a buzzing v. Everything is spoken softly and unhurried, closer to singing than talking.

There is no word for "I"

The smallest pronoun is venn, we-of-two. Glowcaps bud in sibling pairs — Nimbus speaks of himself as "the Nimbus-half of venn." A glowcap who has lost their sibling uses ven-hollo, we-of-two one dark, for the rest of their life.
